Learning together

You're not watching your child play. You're playing too.

A lot of learning apps hand your child a screen and leave you on the side. In Kuwento Quest, the story needs both of you, and you learn the words right alongside your child.

1

You get to be the learner, too

In the story, Lola never taught Nanay the old writing. So you start where many of us actually are: knowing a few words, missing the rest. Your child sees you learning, not just checking their work.

2

Your child hears the words from you

Lola's notebook gives you each word, in Baybayin and in English letters, with how to say it. You read it out loud, and your child hears Tagalog in your voice, not a recording.

3

You figure it out together

Then you both guess what the word means from the picture and a clue. Wrong guesses are part of the game, so your child watches you try, miss, and try again. That's the lesson under the lesson.

Everyone has a job

Seer

Usually a younger child. Finds what's hidden in the pictures.

Scribe

Usually an older child. Decodes Lola's Baybayin pages and writes first.

Word Keeper

Usually a grown-up. Reads Lola's notebook out loud and learns alongside the children.
